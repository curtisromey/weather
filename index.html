What does this mean?

The barometer shows air pressure, the weight of the air pressing down on you. Higher pressure, above about 1020 hPa or 30.1 inHg, usually means settled, dry weather. Lower pressure, below about 1000 hPa or 29.5 inHg, usually means cloud, wind and rain.

hPa and millibars are the same unit. The Met Office uses them. US forecasters mostly use inches of mercury (inHg). 1013 hPa equals 29.92 inHg.

The trend matters more than the number. The dashed needle is where pressure was 3 hours ago. “Falling quickly” or “very rapidly” often means a storm is arriving within hours. The words on the dial are old barometer tradition, a rough guide rather than a forecast.

What does this mean?

Each bar is a 15-minute block. Taller means heavier. Grey means dry.

For scale, over an hour: under 1 mm (0.04 in) is drizzle or a light shower, 1 to 4 mm (0.04 to 0.16 in) is moderate rain, and over 4 mm (0.16 in) is heavy.

What does this mean?

Bars are how much rain is expected each hour, measured on the left scale. The line is the chance of any rain, on the right scale.

A high line with tiny bars means likely but light, think drizzle. A tall bar is a proper downpour. Over 4 mm (0.16 in) in an hour is heavy rain.

Wind and gusts, next 48 hours

Miles per hour

What does this mean?

Wind is the steady average speed. Gusts are the short bursts, and they do the damage.

As a rough guide for gusts: around 40 mph makes walking awkward and knocks bins over. 50 to 60 mph can bring down branches and disrupt travel. Above 70 mph is damaging. On the coast, everything is a notch worse.

What does this mean?

This is the barometer reading over time. Low points are storms passing through. Rises afterwards usually mean clearing skies and often a shift in the wind.

Watch the slope. A steep drop means a deepening storm. When pressure falls about 24 hPa (0.71 inHg) or more in 24 hours, forecasters call it a “weather bomb,” or explosive cyclogenesis.

Is this normal?

Compared with 1991 to 2020 at this time of year

What does this mean?

This compares today with the 30-year average from 1991 to 2020, using days within a week either side of today’s date. That 30-year window is the standard “normal” that weather services use.

“Warmer than 99% of days” means only about 1 in 100 days at this time of year was warmer. That is genuinely unusual.

Do the models agree?

Rain totals for the next 4 days from separate forecast models

What does this mean?

Forecasts come from huge computer simulations called models, and different countries run their own. Met Office is the UK’s, NOAA GFS is the US model, and ECMWF is the European Centre’s, widely regarded as one of the most accurate globally.

When they agree, you can trust the forecast more. When they split, the weather is genuinely uncertain, so expect the forecast to change and check back.

The sea

Waves and swell, next 48 hours

What does this mean?

Wave height here means significant wave height, roughly the average of the biggest third of waves. Individual waves can be nearly twice that.

Period is the seconds between waves. Longer periods, over 10 seconds, carry more power and come from distant storms. Swell is that long-travelled energy, while wind waves are whipped up locally.

The high and low water times are a model estimate. Use the official link for real tide times.

Light

Sunrise, sunset and golden hour, with a rough read on the clouds

What does this mean?

Golden hour is when the sun is low, under 6 degrees above the horizon, giving warm, soft light that is good for photos. In winter at Barrow’s latitude the sun stays low all day, so golden hour stretches out.

The cloud read is a rough guide. High and mid-level cloud tends to catch color at sunrise and sunset. Thick low cloud blocks it.

Aurora watch

Northern lights chances for Barrow tonight

AuroraWatch UK NOAA aurora forecast

What does this mean?

Kp is a 0 to 9 scale of how disturbed Earth’s magnetic field is, which drives the aurora. As a rough rule for Barrow: Kp 5 can bring a glow low on the northern horizon, Kp 6 to 7 can put it overhead, and 4 or under usually needs a camera to catch anything.

AuroraWatch UK is run by Lancaster University using UK magnetometers, so it is the better guide for here. Green means nothing much. Yellow means a camera might catch something in northern England. Amber means it may well be visible by eye in northern England. Red means likely visible across the UK.

You also need darkness and clear skies. Look north, away from streetlights, and try your phone’s night mode, which sees more than your eyes do.

What does this mean?

Storm types by sustained wind: tropical depression under 39 mph, tropical storm 39 to 73 mph, hurricane 74 mph and up. Hurricanes are rated on the Saffir-Simpson scale: Category 1 is 74 to 95 mph, 2 is 96 to 110, 3 is 111 to 129, 4 is 130 to 156, and 5 is 157 and above. Category 3 and up count as major hurricanes.

kt means knots, the unit forecasters use. 1 knot is about 1.15 mph. mb is millibars, the central pressure. The lower it goes, the stronger the storm. Anything within 400 miles of Bartow gets a red edge.

On the outlook map, shaded areas are where a storm might form. Yellow is a low chance, orange medium, and red high, over the next 7 days.
